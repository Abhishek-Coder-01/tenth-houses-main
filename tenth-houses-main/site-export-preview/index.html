02Our Story

We do not rent tents.
We place evenings.

Tenth House began as a favour between two riggers, one in Copenhagen and one in Kyoto, who kept being asked to raise a tent for a wedding nobody else would take on. Seventeen years later we build a small, deliberate register of structures a year, and we still answer that same question first.

  • No catalogue.
  • No stock tents.
  • No poles where you did not ask for them.

A host is introduced to a structure the way a collector is introduced to a painting: with the full dossier, the drawings, and as much time as it takes.

Ishaan Mehra
Founder and Principal
Tenth House, since 2009

Every site starts with a walk, not a catalogue.

Ishaan Mehra learned to rig canvas in Copenhagen and to join timber in Kyoto. He came home with one belief: a structure should belong to its ground, and the ground should be chosen before the drawing is.

In 2009 he raised a tent for a friend's wedding that no one else would take. Seventeen years on, Tenth House builds a small number of structures each year, and Ishaan still walks every site himself.

How the house grew

  1. 2009

    Raises the first tent with a rigger from Kyoto, for a wedding nobody else would take on.

  2. 2014

    Opens the Crosby Street workshop. Drawings, patterns and canvas are all made under one roof.

  3. 2018

    Builds the first glamping suite, in Udaipur, and the pavilion practice begins.

  4. 2022

    Raises a 1,200-guest marquee in 48 hours, struck clean the morning after.

  5. Today

    A deliberate register of 48 structures, each one still walked and signed by Ishaan.

How Ishaan works

  • The site decides

    We stand on the ground first. The structure is drawn only after we know where the light, wind and guests will go.

  • One principal, start to finish

    The person you meet is the person who signs the drawings and checks the last pole.

  • Leave it bare again

    When the last guest has left, we strike the site until nothing shows we were there.
